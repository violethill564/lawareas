📋 Task 1: Client Consultation – Grounds for a Claim

People come to you with all sorts of problems. Decide whether they have the grounds to draft a claim and what area of law their claim falls into.

Client A: "My neighbour's tree fell on my car during a storm. He knew it was diseased but did nothing. I want compensation for the damage."

✅ Yes – grounds exist (negligence: duty of care breached). Area: Law of Tort.

Client B: "I signed a contract to buy a house, but the seller changed their mind and won't complete the sale. I've already paid a deposit."

✅ Yes – breach of contract. Area: Law of Contract (and potentially Land Law for conveyancing aspects).

Client C: "My employer fired me because I'm pregnant. I've only been working there for two months."

✅ Yes – pregnancy discrimination is unlawful regardless of length of service. Area: Employment Law.

Client D: "My business partner is refusing to share the profits even though we have a verbal agreement to split them 50/50."

✅ Yes – partnership dispute. Area: Company Law (partnership law) and/or Law of Contract.

Client E: "Someone copied my novel and published it under their own name. I want them to stop and pay me damages."

✅ Yes – copyright infringement. Area: Intellectual Property Law.

Client F: "I'm going through a divorce and we can't agree on who gets custody of the children."

✅ Yes – child custody dispute. Area: Family Law.

Client G: "I was arrested for shoplifting but I'm innocent. The police didn't read me my rights."

✅ Yes – defence against criminal charges / potential civil claim. Area: Criminal Law (and possibly Law of Tort for false imprisonment).

Client H: "My grandfather left money in a will to be held for my benefit until I turn 25, but my aunt is refusing to hand it over."

✅ Yes – breach of trust. Area: Law of Equity and Trusts.

Task 2: Valid Proof in a Case

Decide whether these documents can be valid proof in a case. Some are valid and some are not.

Document 1: A signed written contract between two parties, dated and witnessed.

✅ Valid proof – A signed, dated, and witnessed contract is strong documentary evidence.

Document 2: A text message from a friend saying "I heard the defendant was at the scene."

❌ Not valid proof – This is hearsay (second-hand information). The friend did not witness the event directly.

Document 3: An unsigned draft of a contract that was never finalised.

❌ Not valid proof – An unsigned draft that was never finalised is not a valid contract and generally cannot serve as proof of agreement.

Document 4: A bank statement showing regular payments from the defendant to the claimant.

✅ Valid proof – Bank statements are official financial records and are admissible as documentary evidence.

Document 5: A photograph taken at the scene of an accident, with metadata showing the date and time.

✅ Valid proof – Photographs with verifiable metadata are strong direct evidence.

Document 6: An email from a lawyer to their client discussing case strategy (marked "confidential").

❌ Not valid proof – Communications between a lawyer and client are protected by legal professional privilege and are generally inadmissible.

Document 7: A birth certificate issued by the government registry office.

✅ Valid proof – A birth certificate is an official government document and is admissible as proof of identity/age.

Document 8: A verbal agreement recalled by one party, with no written record or witnesses.

❌ Not valid proof – A verbal agreement with no written record or witnesses is extremely difficult to prove in court.
